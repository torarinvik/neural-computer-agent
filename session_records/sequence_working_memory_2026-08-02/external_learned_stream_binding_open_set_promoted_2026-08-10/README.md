# Open-set learned anonymous binding

This archive records the promoted open-set extension of the learned
anonymous stream-binding boundary. It is a transport and memory-lifecycle
pressure test, not a claim of general continual learning.

The audit trains one generic transition-context encoder from paired views,
freezes it, and deploys an external binding memory with three live slots and
two bounded provisional slots. Four anonymous streams are presented. The
fourth stream arrives after live capacity is full and is accumulated as a
provisional identity through six irregularly timed observations. It cannot
emit a live key or mutate the existing live tracks until a retention-gated
retirement and promotion succeed.

Across seeds `2301` and `2302`:

- the open-set encoder converged after 240 optimizer updates per seed;
- the fourth stream remained provisional for all six arrivals;
- irregular delay estimates, exact provisional persistence, and frozen
  controller state passed;
- rejected retirement and rejected admission left the binding state unchanged;
- verified retirement followed by promotion admitted the new stream while
  retaining the sibling tracks;
- deployment used zero replay and zero controller optimizer updates.

The raw reports are `report_seed2301.json` and `report_seed2302.json`.
`sample_efficiency_ledger.json` records the two-seed accounting; `SHA256SUMS`
protects the archived reports and ledger.

## Claim boundary

This promotes bounded learned open-set quarantine, delay/reliability state,
and retention-safe replacement. It does not establish learned eviction or
admission policy, arbitrary identity formation, unrestricted memory growth,
general drift handling, natural-language grounding, or general continual
learning. The next experiment should train the admission/retention challenger
from outcomes and stress multiple simultaneous provisional identities,
contradiction, drift, and capacity pressure without caller-designed probes.
